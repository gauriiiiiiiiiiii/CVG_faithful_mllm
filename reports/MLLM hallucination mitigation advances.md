# Training-free hallucination mitigation accelerates past dual-pass decoding

The training-free MLLM hallucination mitigation field has tripled from 24 methods in 2024 to **79 methods by October 2026** ([Awesome Train-Free list](https://github.com/zhongjingyu1/Awesome-Train-Free-MLLM-LVLM-Hallucination-Mitigation)), and the dominant technical trend is a shift from dual-forward-pass contrastive decoding toward single-pass attention reweighting and activation steering. Methods like AttnReal (IJCV 2026) achieve near-zero overhead at **2 extra GFLOPs per token** while matching or exceeding VCD's hallucination reduction ([AttnReal](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08342v2)). Adaptive token selection has advanced through entropy-based budgeting (AdaptToken), head-level steering (ASCD), and variance-weighted scoring (VisionTrim), though no method yet combines entropy-adaptive alpha with CVG's cross+self attention formula. On standard benchmarks, AGLA (CVPR 2025) leads POPE adversarial at **81.13% accuracy** and PAI (ECCV 2024) leads CHAIR with **CHAIR_S 24.8** on LLaVA-1.5-7B, while CVG's exact numbers remain difficult to compare due to the absence of a unified evaluation protocol. The most significant unsolved problem for training-free methods is the TextVQA regression: attention-based token selection disproportionately discards text-containing patches, with **up to 42% TextVQA accuracy loss** at 75% pruning ([FEATHER](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13180v2)). A NeurIPS 2025 critique further challenges the field by showing that some contrastive decoding gains on POPE reflect distribution skewing rather than genuine faithfulness improvement ([Mirage](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/2f89a23a19d1617e7fb16d4f7a049ce2-Abstract-Conference.html)).

## Single-pass methods eliminate contrastive decoding's dual-pass cost

The first generation of training-free hallucination mitigation -- VCD (CVPR 2024), ICD, and M3ID -- all require a second forward pass through the MLLM to produce a contrastive distribution, roughly doubling inference cost. VCD runs at **1.83x baseline latency** on V100 and **214 GFLOPs per token** compared to greedy decoding's 102 ([DaID](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10071); [AttnReal](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08342v2)). CVG, which generates output from a masked visual input branch, likely incurs a similar ~2x overhead, though no paper reports its exact cost.

Three distinct strategies now achieve contrastive-decoding-like effects within a single forward pass. **AttnReal** (IJCV 2026) detects attention sinks -- output tokens absorbing disproportionate attention (a_j > T/n) -- and redistributes the freed attention uniformly to visual tokens. The overhead is **104 GFLOPs per token versus 102 for greedy**, effectively zero. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, AttnReal drops **CHAIR_S from 47.0 to 30.6** and **CHAIR_I from 14.3 to 7.0** ([AttnReal](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08342v2)). **DaID** (Dual-Anchor Introspective Decoding, 2026) reuses intermediate hidden states from the standard forward pass, identifying a "Spotlight layer" with maximum visual attention and a "Shadow layer" with minimum visual attention, then contrasting their projected logits. It runs at **1.31-1.35x baseline latency**, compared to VCD's 1.83x on the same hardware ([DaID](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10071)). **ASCD** (AAAI 2026) takes a different approach entirely: it identifies text-centric attention heads offline (top-32 heads by text-to-visual attention ratio over 500 images), then at inference amplifies these heads and dampens attention to the top 10% of visual tokens. The final distribution follows the contrastive form p_final = (1+alpha) * p_pos_steered - alpha * p_neg_steered. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, ASCD achieves **CHAIR_S 35.6** and reports **up to 38.2% hallucination reduction** across POPE, CHAIR, and MMHal-Bench ([ASCD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14766v2); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38000)).

A fourth approach, **ECD** (ECML PKDD 2025), replaces the contrastive distribution with a lightweight meta-classifier (logistic regression or gradient boosting) trained on internal model features such as per-layer NLL, KL divergence between layers, and image-attention entropy. ECD matches greedy latency on POPE (**0.7s per question versus VCD's 1.3s**) while achieving **POPE F1 89.28 versus VCD's 88.49** on LLaVA-1.5 ([ECD](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12137v1)). The caveat is that ECD requires pre-training its classifier on 5,000 labeled images, adding a one-time setup cost. Two additional methods round out the landscape: **VISTA** (ICML 2025) uses a Visual Steering Vector to restore visual cues in activation space plus self-logits augmentation from early layers, reporting **~40% hallucination reduction** across four benchmarks and four architectures ([VISTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03628)), and **OTT** (2026) manipulates vision tokens at intermediate layers by concatenating augmented image tokens with originals and blending via attention, achieving **POPE F1 87.04** at only **1.06x overhead** ([OTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10360)).

The evolution is clear: contrastive signals that once required a full second forward pass can now be extracted from the model's own internal representations -- intermediate layer states (DaID), attention head structure (ASCD), or attention weight distribution (AttnReal) -- at a fraction of the cost.

## Adaptive token selection advances on three fronts but leaves CVG's formula unexplored

CVG scores visual tokens as a = a_cross + a_self, using the sum of cross-attention and self-attention to partition tokens into complementary and supplementary branches ([CVG, IEEE TIP 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/tip.2025.3644140)). No published method has proposed a weighted variant of this formula (e.g., w1 * a_cross + w2 * a_self) or normalized it, making this a clear research gap. Instead, the field has advanced adaptive selection along three orthogonal axes.

**Entropy-based budgeting** determines how many tokens to keep rather than which ones. **AdaptToken** (2026) uses response entropy as a global signal for token budgeting in video MLLMs: each frame group's budget is proportional to Softmax(C_g / tau), where C_g is the negative mean entropy of the lowest-10% tokens and tau = 2. More confident (lower-entropy) groups receive more tokens. On Qwen2.5-VL 7B, it boosts VideoMME from 65.4 to **70.5** and MLVU from 70.2 to **76.8** ([AdaptToken](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28696)). **QTSplus** (2025) similarly uses the entropy of normalized cross-attention relevance distributions to set per-frame token budgets ([QTSplus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11910)). These methods address the budget question adaptively but remain oriented toward video -- adapting entropy-based budgeting to image-domain hallucination mitigation (using response entropy to set pruning aggressiveness per image) is an unexplored extension.

**Head-level and spatial-aware scoring** replaces uniform attention aggregation with structured selection. ASCD's offline identification of text-centric versus object-centric attention heads (described above) demonstrates that different heads attend to different patch types, and weighting them uniformly loses information ([ASCD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14766v2)). **AdaptPrune** (2025) reframes visual token pruning as Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), combining attention scores with spatial distance and token similarity through an adaptive suppression step ([AdaptPrune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08019)). **AgilePruner** (2026) proposes an adaptive similarity threshold tied to image complexity, pruning candidate tokens whose cosine distance to the selected token falls below the threshold ([AgilePruner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01236)).

**Visual-only versus text-guided scoring** challenges CVG's inclusion of cross-attention. **VisionDrop** (AAAI 2025) scores tokens using only visual self-attention from the vision encoder and reports that **visual-only scoring generally beats text-guided scoring, especially under aggressive pruning** ([VisionDrop](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39949/43910)). This directly questions the rationale for including cross-attention in CVG's formula, though the comparison was tested for efficiency rather than hallucination mitigation specifically. **VisionTrim** (2026) combines [CLS] token attention from the vision encoder with a variance-based adaptive weighting as a second signal, the closest published analog to a weighted attention combination ([VisionTrim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22674)). The separation between scoring mechanisms (which tokens) and budgeting mechanisms (how many tokens) is a useful decomposition that no existing method fully exploits in the hallucination mitigation context.

## Benchmark landscape reveals no single dominant method and raises evaluation concerns

Direct cross-paper comparison of training-free methods is unreliable because no unified benchmark protocol exists -- papers vary in backbone model, decoding strategy, token budget, and evaluation split. The strongest verified results on LLaVA-1.5-7B come from individual papers' own comparison tables.

On **POPE**, AGLA (CVPR 2025) achieves the highest verified per-split numbers: **88.54 / 87.71 (Acc/F1) on random** and **81.13 / 81.36 on adversarial**, compared to VCD's 86.84/86.83 random and 77.31/79.28 adversarial, and vanilla's 83.49/82.28 random and 76.03/76.26 adversarial ([AGLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12718v3)). DeCo (ICLR 2025) reports a competitive **POPE F1 of 86.7** in a greedy setting ([DeCo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11779)), and ECD reaches **89.28** ([ECD](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12137v1)). On **CHAIR** (lower is better), PAI (ECCV 2024) shows the strongest improvement with **CHAIR_S 24.8 versus 46.6 baseline** in greedy decoding, a ~47% relative reduction ([PAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21771v1)). AttnReal achieves **CHAIR_S 30.6** (versus 47.0 baseline), and AGLA reaches **43.0** with multinomial sampling (versus 51.0 baseline) while maintaining **recall 78.9** versus 75.2 baseline ([AGLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12718v3)). On **AMBER**, data is sparse: DeCo drops CHAIR from 8.2 to **6.6** and hallucination rate from 34.3 to **28.1** as reproduced by the LISA paper ([LISA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19110)). CRoPS (TMLR 2026) claims "lowest CHAIR scores across all LVLMs" with 15-25% relative reduction versus M3ID, but absolute numbers were not extractable from available snippets ([CRoPS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00659)). AttnReal (IJCV 2026) claims best hallucination performance across most model/decoding settings on CHAIR ([AttnReal](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08342)).

On **MME Perception**, AGLA leads with **1472.66** versus 1363.96 for VCD and 1279.19 for the regular baseline, a **193-point gain** ([AGLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12718v3)). The field notably lacks consolidated **TextVQA** comparisons for hallucination-mitigated methods; most papers omit this benchmark, and those that include it show consistent degradation (discussed in the next section).

A critical reassessment published in September 2026 challenges the reliability of POPE as an evaluation metric. Fazli et al. evaluate six inference-time methods across three LVLMs and find that **methods that cut hallucination also cut object recall**: on LLaVA-1.5, CEI drops CHAIR_S from 46.6 to 31.0 but recall drops from 79.6 to 68.6, and VCD actually raises CHAIR_S from 46.6 to **53.8** (worse) while maintaining recall at 80.0 ([Fazli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01888)). Separately, the "Mirage of Performance Gains" paper (NeurIPS 2025) demonstrates that spurious methods that do not target hallucination at all achieve comparable POPE gains to contrastive decoding, because contrastive inputs push outputs toward "No," and the adaptive plausibility constraint reduces sampling to near-greedy. VCD **lowers MME-P from 1475.1 to 1389.9** and **raises CHAIR_S from 21.6 to 25.4** (worse) on LLaVA-1.5-7B under their evaluation ([Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10020v3)). These findings are directly relevant to CVG's contrastive approach: contrasting complementary versus supplementary token branches may introduce a similar bias toward conservative generation. Methods that maintain high recall while reducing hallucination -- like AGLA with recall 78.9 versus vanilla 75.2 -- have stronger claims to genuine faithfulness improvement.

## Text-rich images remain training-free methods' unsolved weakness

No paper directly addresses CVG's TextVQA regression by name, but the underlying mechanism is well-documented across the visual token pruning literature. Text-containing patches are systematically disadvantaged by attention-based token selection because text information is **spatially distributed** across many small patches rather than concentrated in large contiguous regions like objects. When attention scores determine which tokens to keep, text patches receive lower aggregate scores and are disproportionately classified as irrelevant.

**FEATHER** (2025) provides the most rigorous diagnosis. At 75% pruning with FastV's attention-based criterion, **TextVQA accuracy drops 42.0%**, far exceeding drops on other benchmarks. The root cause is RoPE's long-term decay, which inflates attention scores for tokens later in raster-scan order (bottom of image): the average retained position sits at **80.7% down the image**, systematically discarding top-of-image text patches. FEATHER's fix -- removing RoPE from the selection criterion and ensembling with uniform spatial sampling -- **recovers TextVQA from 31.8 to 51.7** at aggressive compression, against a 54.9 baseline ([FEATHER](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13180v2)). Prior works report smaller TextVQA declines only because they inject OCR-parsed tokens into prompts, reducing reliance on visual information -- when OCR tokens are excluded (the fair evaluation protocol), pruning's TextVQA impact is severe.

The discovery of **specialized OCR attention heads** opens a promising direction. Research published at EMNLP 2025 identifies attention heads that specifically attend to image patches containing text and read them out, with an "OCR score" measuring how consistently a head retrieves text from image regions ([OCR Heads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15865)). ASCD exploits related findings by **amplifying text-centric heads and dampening others**, achieving hallucination reduction on POPE and CHAIR **while improving accuracy on TextVQA** -- the only training-free method reported to do so ([ASCD](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38000/41962)). This suggests that CVG's a = a_cross + a_self formula could be decomposed further: different heads attend to different patch types (text versus object), and weighting them uniformly may lose critical text information.

Several spatial-diversity methods partially mitigate the problem without targeting text specifically. **GridPrune** (2025) allocates token budgets across spatial zones before local selection, ensuring text regions in low-attention zones still receive representation ([GridPrune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10081v1)). **D2Pruner** (AAAI 2026) debiases attention scores then selects diverse tokens using a graph combining spatial adjacency and semantic similarity ([D2Pruner](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38234/42196)). **PoRe** (2025) corrects positional bias in attention-based pruning as a plug-and-play training-free module, with largest gains at aggressive pruning ratios ([PoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17807v1)). **TokenCarve** (2025) achieves the smallest TextVQA drop at 64 tokens: **57.0 versus 58.2 baseline** (only -1.2 points), suggesting its SVD-based information-preservation score helps text patches specifically, though the authors do not claim this ([TokenCarve](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10501v1)).

For training-free OCR hallucination mitigation specifically, the gap remains open. "Seeing is Believing?" (NeurIPS 2025) is the first work to directly target OCR hallucination, introducing the KIE-HVQA benchmark and achieving **58.05 average score versus GPT-4o's 30.21** after cold-start SFT followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL-7B -- but the method requires fine-tuning, incompatible with CVG's training-free philosophy ([Seeing is Believing?](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20168v2)). No training-free method was found that specifically targets text-rich image hallucination mitigation. An explicit text-versus-object patch classifier based on local attention connectivity patterns (without external OCR) appears to be an unexplored research direction.

## Conclusion

The most actionable finding for extending CVG is that single-pass contrastive approximations have matured enough to replace dual-pass architectures without sacrificing hallucination reduction quality. DaID's layer-contrasting and ASCD's head-steering both demonstrate that the contrastive signal CVG extracts through a masked visual branch can instead be derived from the model's internal representations at 1.0-1.35x cost rather than ~2x. The absence of any work exploring weighted combinations of cross-attention and self-attention (a = w_cross * a_cross + w_self * a_self with learned or adaptive weights) represents a surprisingly clean gap -- especially given VisionDrop's finding that self-attention alone outperforms cross-attention for token importance scoring. For CVG's TextVQA regression, the most promising training-free path combines two insights: ASCD's text-centric head amplification (which improves TextVQA while reducing hallucination) and FEATHER's RoPE debiasing (which recovers most TextVQA performance lost to attention-based pruning). A CVG extension that identifies text-centric versus object-centric heads, weights their attention contributions differently in the scoring formula, and applies positional debiasing before the complementary/supplementary split would address an unoccupied niche. Finally, evaluation methodology is due for a reckoning: the NeurIPS 2025 "Mirage" critique and the 2026 "risk suppression" analysis together demonstrate that POPE gains alone are insufficient evidence of genuine faithfulness, and any new method must report both precision (CHAIR, POPE) and recall/coverage (object recall, AMBER coverage) metrics to make a credible claim.
